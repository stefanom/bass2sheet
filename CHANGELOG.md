# Changelog

## 0.2.0 (unreleased)

Measured against ground truth for the first time; the numbers are in `docs/evaluation.md`.

- **Judging on your own songs.** `bass2sheet-judge` takes your songs through the pipeline one step at a time: the isolated bass tracks, the notes heard in them, the count, the written rhythm, the tab. Each step sets the tool's choice against its alternative on short passages, by ear and by eye, without saying which version is which, and is built on what you picked at the steps before it. A cursor follows the audio over the pictures and scores, and what differs is marked. Picks decide the defaults, and a judged passage comes back when a later change alters it. See `docs/judging.md`.
- **The copilot.** `bass2sheet-copilot` puts the person in charge of the score. A song plays through on a timeline with the isolated bass as a pitch picture, the tool's notes over it and played in the bass's own sound; the person stops at a mistake and fixes it: a guess taken with one key, a nudge, a note deleted, added, split or joined. After a fix the tool offers the other notes with the same label that sound the same. What was left untouched counts as right as far as the person has worked (up to their last fix, or to where they say they have checked: people listen ahead), the sheet is written from the person's notes, and every fix is kept as the tool's record of a mistake (`report`). The picture locates each partial exactly in pitch and time (a reassigned spectrogram of the steady tones), so quick low notes a semitone apart show as separate bars. The song can be played at 90 to 50% of its speed at the same pitch, with the notes in step, and the timeline is dragged with the mouse. On the evaluation sets the right pitch is the first guess for 59% of wrong notes on real bass alone and among the first four for 74%. See `docs/copilot.md`.
- **The song's harmony.** A song's chords are found eighth note by eighth note, with the bass notes as evidence for the root and sevenths beside triads, and its key or keys are read from the chords (`harmony.py`). Before, the tool wrote one major or minor triad a bar and one key. On 24 songs of RWC Popular the root is right 0.86 of the time (0.73 before), root and major or minor 0.86 (0.72), root and seventh quality 0.68 (0.50); the main key on 24 songs of 24 (19). Each chord has a score that is the measured share of such chords that are right, and each key the next most likely key beside it. The sheet's chord symbols come from it, with a second symbol on beat 3 where the chord changes in the bar, seventh chords, and the bass under a chord. Stored with every song as `harmony` (in `notes.json`, and in the copilot's `machine.json`), beside the chord per bar and the key that older data has and older code reads; a song added to the copilot before gets it when it is added again. The weights are fitted on BabySlakh (CC BY 4.0), not on the songs it is measured on. See `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **The harmony of a song.** A page for each song in the copilot shows how the song is built, to understand it and remember it: the few loops its bass line goes round (found from the note the line stands on in each bar), what the bass plays in each as steps of a home you can change, and the shape those notes make on the neck. The chords are the person's to write, not the tool's: a click on a beat, or a tap along with the song, says where a chord starts; the tool offers six chords to try there, ranked by the bass notes, the key, what it hears and the chords put at the same place in other rounds; a click puts one on the beat and plays that stretch of the record with it. A round of chords can be copied to the rounds that have none. The chords are shown by the job they do in the key (home, away, tension) on a map that is also a way to pick one, on the circle of fifths and on a circle of semitones, and can be heard in a keyboard sound over the record. The score and the play-along page carry the chords the person wrote and no others. On songs whose chords are known, the chord wanted is among the six offered for 98% of the changes on the RWC Popular test songs, and for 75% to 97% on three commercial recordings held against MIDI transcriptions (`dev/chord_offers_eval.py`). See [docs/copilot.md](docs/copilot.md#the-harmony-of-a-song).
- **A fingering for playing.** The copilot now puts a song on the neck so that the hand moves as little as it can (`easy` in `score.frets`): a hand position that reaches four frets, moves made where there is time, few string skips, open strings where they save a move, the same fingering for a bar each time it comes. The harmony page shows it with the hand drawn on the neck, says how many hand moves each way costs over the song, and lets you choose per song between it, the same without open strings (`closed`) and the old one nearest the nut (`low`); the score's tab and the play-along page follow. The one-shot `bass2sheet` command still writes `low` unless told otherwise.
- **A demo film.** Four minutes, one song from the recording to a sheet fixed by hand, then to playing along: [stefanom.github.io/bass2sheet](https://stefanom.github.io/bass2sheet/). It is made by a script from the real pages (`dev/demo/`, [docs/demo.md](docs/demo.md)); the film itself is a file of the release `demo-film`, not of the repository.
- **Tests that hold the code.** The default tests reach 97% of the lines and branches, and CI fails under 95%; they need no MuseScore, no model and no network (stand-ins take their place). Twelve more drive every page in a real browser with clicks and keys (`uv run pytest -m browser`). CI runs on Linux and macOS, and runs `./bootstrap` on a fresh Mac.
- **Fixed, found by those tests.** The first page lost a song's picture now and then when it asked for all of them at once. A score made before was forgotten when MuseScore failed on the next try. The page servers printed an error whenever a browser closed a connection early. `serve --port 0` printed port 0 in place of the port it got.
- **Getting started on a Mac.** `./bootstrap` takes a fresh clone to a self-check that passes: Homebrew's packages from the `Brewfile`, the Python environment from `uv.lock`, the model files, then `bass2sheet-doctor`, which says for each thing the tool needs whether it is there, what is lost without it and the command that fixes it. `docs/install.md` has every step by hand and what was measured.
- **The copilot adds a song without the beat model.** When the Beat This! model cannot be loaded (not downloaded yet and no network, or its server is down) and you have not asked for it by name, the built-in beat tracker finds the beats, and the song's "Beats and bars" step says so. Before, the song was refused.
- **`BASS2SHEET_CACHE`** moves the cache (models, separated tracks, pitch tracks) for every command; `--cache-dir` still does it for one.
- **One note of a pair.** Where two notes are played together, the copilot's page lets each be clicked, deleted or moved by itself; before, a pair could only be deleted whole.
- **MuScriptor's second notes have to be heard.** With `--notes muscriptor`, notes it starts together were all written together. On the test songs 2 of 79 (RWC Popular) and 3 of 120 (BabySlakh) of those second notes are in the answer key. Now one of them is the note (the one bass2sheet's finder hears too, else the lowest; of two a step apart, the one that sounds), and another is written with it only where bass2sheet's own finder wrote the same two.
- **Playing along on a phone asks for a key.** `serve --lan` no longer serves every device on the network: a device shows the eight-digit key at the end of the link the copilot prints and shows on its first page, and keeps a cookie from then on. Addresses a phone cannot reach (a hotspot's) are no longer printed, and nothing is answered beyond a home network's addresses.
- **Somebody's transcription as an answer key.** `bass2sheet-copilot reference SONG FILE` lines a MIDI file (or, through MuseScore, a Guitar Pro tab or a MusicXML score) up with a song's recording and keeps its bass part as an answer key; `report` scores the tool's notes and your version against it (`reference.py`). The line-up finds the file's key and tempo, jumps over what one of the two lacks, and settles the timing on the recording's onsets. `dev/reference_eval.py` measures it where the truth is known.
- **The test songs, to hear and to rate.** `bass2sheet-copilot testset DATASET --scores NAME=RUN_DIR` adds the songs a `bass2sheet-eval` run measured to the copilot, and its page `/tests` plays each one (the record or the isolated bass, from its busiest stretch), says what kind of recording it is, and takes an answer: like my music, partly, not like it. The scores are then counted by those answers (`testsongs.py`; `report` prints the table).
- **Repeats.** The copilot finds the bars that sound alike, in the bass track and not in the notes (`repeats.py`), and names them over the timeline. A fix is offered to the other bars that sound the same at that spot and are written as the fixed bar was; a bar written unlike a repeat that sounds the same is marked with what the repeat has, to take or leave. Against ground truth, carrying fixes saves about a sixth of the fixing (a few percent on most songs, more than half on some), and a carried pitch or re-pluck is right about nine times in ten. See `docs/copilot.md` and `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **The copilot's songs, and a way in for a new one.** The copilot opens on the songs of the working directory: each with its pitch picture as a strip, how much of it is worked through, how many fixes, and whose notes it started from. A new song is added there by dropping an audio file: it is kept under the working directory and prepared in the background by the code `add` runs, from bass2sheet's notes or MuScriptor's, while the page shows each step as waiting, running or done, with its time and what it found (`common.step`, told by the pipeline itself). A song's page is now at `/song`; old links still open it. The server stays on this computer and on the standard library. The page shows the way from a recording to its score in four stages (add, watch it being prepared, fix the notes, make the score), names the double checks among the steps and says what the running step does. See `docs/copilot.md`.
- **A song from a link.** Where yt-dlp is installed, the copilot's first page also takes a YouTube link: the audio is fetched under the working directory and prepared like a file that was dropped, with the fetching as the first step shown. Optional, nothing is installed, and it is for audio you have the right to download.
- **The score, seen in the copilot.** "Make the sheet" now opens the score in a panel over the song's page: MuseScore draws a picture of each page along with the PDF, and the panel shows them with the PDF, the MusicXML and the MIDI file to download. Without MuseScore the MusicXML is still written and the panel says so. Every score made is kept as a version (when, how many fixes, how much checked by ear); the panel switches between them and downloads each.
- **The notes checked by the bass's own sound.** Every note found is a sample of this bass at that pitch, so each note is compared with the song's other notes of its pitch (`ownsound.py`). A note they do not explain, or that another pitch explains better, goes in parentheses and on the list of bars to check; the share of such notes is a new gate, reported but not yet part of the verdict. `audio/bass_rebuilt.wav` is the transcription played with those samples, each note by a sample cut from another note. `bass2sheet-judge check` runs the comparison on the judged songs without anyone listening and keeps a history. Against ground truth the share of such notes tracks a song's accuracy (rank correlation −0.85), and about two in three of the notes it marks are wrong; it finds a quarter of the wrong notes in a mix. See `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **Lines that jump are no longer written too low.** The pitch path can leap between notes; it could only move smoothly, so after a jump of a fifth or more the note before was held over the new one. On real bass guitar the share of such notes written right went from 46% to 79%, and slap pops from 18% to 72%. When the two pitch trackers are an octave apart and CREPE is confident, its octave stands. Found by judging: the timing was right and the notes were too low.
- **No more blips between notes.** The slide from one note to the next, the pitch sinking as a note is let go and the thump of a pluck were written as notes of 70 to 130 ms a step or two away. They now join the note they belong to. Precision on RWC Popular 0.71 to 0.73. The cost, measured on MDB-bass-synth's exact pitch curves: about a fifth of what is no longer written there was a real note of some 80 ms (a hammer-on, a grace note).
- **A faint octave under a note is not the note.** When the pitch tracker is sure of the lower of two octaves, that note now has to be loud or to have a partial of its own (its third); otherwise the upper one is written. Eight notes of one song had gone an octave low. Where both octaves really sound, the lower is written.
- **Nothing below the instrument.** What is heard below E1, the lowest string of the four-string bass the tab is written for, is written an octave up: an octave effect or a synth under the bass put 75 of one song's 1,166 notes there. `--lowest B0|D1|none` for other instruments.
- **Notes played together.** Where the bass plays two notes at once, both are written: a chord on the staff and in the tab, in the MIDI and in the playback. The pitch trackers follow one note at a time and wrote the upper one, pieces of both, or nothing. Every stretch is now looked at again by its spectral peaks (`together.py`). An octave is never written as two notes. `--together single` turns it off, and the judging page has a step for it.
- **After a change, `bass2sheet-judge update` builds again what is not settled**: passages still open, or where neither version was right, if the tool writes them differently now.
- **Pitch cache.** The cache holds CREPE's bin probabilities (`crepe-*.npz`) instead of the decoded pitch, so the decoding can change without running the model again. Older `tracks-*.npz` files are no longer read and can be deleted.
- **MuScriptor's notes, if you want them.** `--notes muscriptor` (on `bass2sheet`, `bass2sheet-copilot add` and `bass2sheet-eval`) writes MuScriptor's notes for the isolated bass in place of bass2sheet's own (`muscriptor.py`). They are made into one line, their starts move to the plucks, and bass2sheet's own notes become the second opinion: a note MuScriptor writes where bass2sheet hears another pitch, or nothing, or a held note with no pluck, is flagged. On RWC Popular the flagged notes are 11% of the notes and 56% of them are wrong. Optional: a program built separately (`dev/muscriptor_cpp`) and weights with a non-commercial licence, see `docs/muscriptor.md`. The default has not changed.
- **Measured against MuScriptor.** `dev/muscriptor_eval.py` runs Kyutai and Mirelo's transcription model on the same recordings (through its C++ port: `dev/muscriptor_cpp`; or the reference code: `dev/muscriptor_runner.py`). Given the bass bass2sheet isolates, it is the better note finder: F 0.91 against 0.79 on real bass played alone, 0.83 against 0.76 on RWC Popular. Given the full mix it is worse than separating first (0.72 on RWC, 0.50 on BabySlakh), because it mislabels instruments. bass2sheet does not use it; its weights are non-commercial.
- **Re-plucks measured in the original recording.** `dev/repluck_eval.py`: a string plucked again shows a larger rise in level in the original than in the isolated bass (8.7 dB against 4.8), but so do other instruments. A rise in both finds 36% of the re-plucks at 3% false alarms, the isolated bass alone 14%, bass2sheet's whole finder 59%, MuScriptor on the isolated bass 86%. Not used by the tool.
- **Measured on real bass guitar.** `dev/idmt_eval.py` runs the note finder on IDMT-SMT-Bass-Single-Track, by plucking style, beside Basic Pitch and YourMT3+ (`dev/mt3_runner.py`). Neither does better; see `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **Choices you can make per run.** `--replucks every|clear`, `--rhythm literal|simple` (stray sixteenths onto eighths, short rests closed) and `--tab low|position` (the hand kept in one position).
- **Line times** in the score follow the tracked beats; they assumed a steady tempo before.
- **File names.** "(Official Video)" and the like are dropped from the title and the output folder.
- **Evaluation.** `bass2sheet-eval` scores the tool on BabySlakh/Slakh2100 (exact notes and beats), MDB-bass-synth (exact f0 on real mixes) and RWC Popular (aligned MIDI on real recordings), with an error breakdown, a gate-vs-accuracy table and a baseline regression check.
- **Note starts** move to where the pluck begins to sound, at 1 ms resolution. Frame-level starts were 15–25 ms late.
- **Beats and downbeats** come from the Beat This! model. The built-in tracker chose the wrong pulse level on about half of the BabySlakh songs.
- **Repeated notes.** A held note is split where the string is plucked again. Driving eighth-note lines came out as single long notes on real recordings.
- **Separator choice.** When both separators were transcribed, the result with fewer failed gates is kept, then the one with more note starts confirmed by Basic Pitch. The report says which gates each attempt tripped.
- **Quality gates.** Thresholds are set from the evaluation runs, and five warnings at once count as a failure. The drum-bleed and synth-gap gates are gone: they fired on good results. A meter gate (downbeats that fit 4/4) is new; it and the eighth-note gate warn but never fail. Note coverage no longer penalizes a perfect transcription.
- **Memory.** CREPE's batch is bounded (it could need over 30 GB), and cached GPU memory is released between stages.
- **Inputs.** `--stems DIR` uses stems you already have. `--bass-stem`, `--beat-tracker`, `--crepe-model`, `--no-pdf`. The shortest accepted input is 10 s.
- **Score.** Lines start where the bass comes in.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for every stage and end-to-end tests on a synthesized song, with no recorded audio and no model download; an opt-in tier for real separation and the dataset regression.
