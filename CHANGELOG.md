# Changelog

## 0.2.0 (unreleased)

Measured against ground truth for the first time; the numbers are in `docs/evaluation.md`.

- **Judging on your own songs.** `bass2sheet-judge` takes your songs through the pipeline one step at a time: the isolated bass tracks, the notes heard in them, the count, the written rhythm, the tab. Each step sets the tool's choice against its alternative on short passages, by ear and by eye, without saying which version is which, and is built on what you picked at the steps before it. A cursor follows the audio over the pictures and scores, and what differs is marked. Picks decide the defaults, and a judged passage comes back when a later change alters it. See `docs/judging.md`.
- **The copilot.** `bass2sheet-copilot` puts the person in charge of the score. A song plays through on a timeline with the isolated bass as a pitch picture, the tool's notes over it and played in the bass's own sound; the person stops at a mistake and fixes it: a guess taken with one key, a nudge, a note deleted, added, split or joined. After a fix the tool offers the other notes with the same label that sound the same. What was left untouched counts as right as far as the person has worked (up to their last fix, or to where they say they have checked: people listen ahead), the sheet is written from the person's notes, and every fix is kept as the tool's record of a mistake (`report`). The picture locates each partial exactly in pitch and time (a reassigned spectrogram of the steady tones), so quick low notes a semitone apart show as separate bars. The song can be played at 90 to 50% of its speed at the same pitch, with the notes in step, and the timeline is dragged with the mouse. On the evaluation sets the right pitch is the first guess for 59% of wrong notes on real bass alone and among the first four for 74%. See `docs/copilot.md`.
- **Repeats.** The copilot finds the bars that sound alike, in the bass track and not in the notes (`repeats.py`), and names them over the timeline. A fix is offered to the other bars that sound the same at that spot and are written as the fixed bar was; a bar written unlike a repeat that sounds the same is marked with what the repeat has, to take or leave. Against ground truth, carrying fixes saves about a sixth of the fixing (a few percent on most songs, more than half on some), and a carried pitch or re-pluck is right about nine times in ten. See `docs/copilot.md` and `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **The notes checked by the bass's own sound.** Every note found is a sample of this bass at that pitch, so each note is compared with the song's other notes of its pitch (`ownsound.py`). A note they do not explain, or that another pitch explains better, goes in parentheses and on the list of bars to check; the share of such notes is a new gate, reported but not yet part of the verdict. `audio/bass_rebuilt.wav` is the transcription played with those samples, each note by a sample cut from another note. `bass2sheet-judge check` runs the comparison on the judged songs without anyone listening and keeps a history. Against ground truth the share of such notes tracks a song's accuracy (rank correlation −0.85), and about two in three of the notes it marks are wrong; it finds a quarter of the wrong notes in a mix. See `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **Lines that jump are no longer written too low.** The pitch path can leap between notes; it could only move smoothly, so after a jump of a fifth or more the note before was held over the new one. On real bass guitar the share of such notes written right went from 46% to 79%, and slap pops from 18% to 72%. When the two pitch trackers are an octave apart and CREPE is confident, its octave stands. Found by judging: the timing was right and the notes were too low.
- **No more blips between notes.** The slide from one note to the next, the pitch sinking as a note is let go and the thump of a pluck were written as notes of 70 to 130 ms a step or two away. They now join the note they belong to. Precision on RWC Popular 0.71 to 0.73. The cost, measured on MDB-bass-synth's exact pitch curves: about a fifth of what is no longer written there was a real note of some 80 ms (a hammer-on, a grace note).
- **A faint octave under a note is not the note.** When the pitch tracker is sure of the lower of two octaves, that note now has to be loud or to have a partial of its own (its third); otherwise the upper one is written. Eight notes of one song had gone an octave low. Where both octaves really sound, the lower is written.
- **Nothing below the instrument.** What is heard below E1, the lowest string of the four-string bass the tab is written for, is written an octave up: an octave effect or a synth under the bass put 75 of one song's 1,166 notes there. `--lowest B0|D1|none` for other instruments.
- **Notes played together.** Where the bass plays two notes at once, both are written: a chord on the staff and in the tab, in the MIDI and in the playback. The pitch trackers follow one note at a time and wrote the upper one, pieces of both, or nothing. Every stretch is now looked at again by its spectral peaks (`together.py`). An octave is never written as two notes. `--together single` turns it off, and the judging page has a step for it.
- **After a change, `bass2sheet-judge update` builds again what is not settled**: passages still open, or where neither version was right, if the tool writes them differently now.
- **Pitch cache.** The cache holds CREPE's bin probabilities (`crepe-*.npz`) instead of the decoded pitch, so the decoding can change without running the model again. Older `tracks-*.npz` files are no longer read and can be deleted.
- **Measured on real bass guitar.** `dev/idmt_eval.py` runs the note finder on IDMT-SMT-Bass-Single-Track, by plucking style, beside Basic Pitch and YourMT3+ (`dev/mt3_runner.py`). Neither does better; see `docs/evaluation.md`.
- **Choices you can make per run.** `--replucks every|clear`, `--rhythm literal|simple` (stray sixteenths onto eighths, short rests closed) and `--tab low|position` (the hand kept in one position).
- **Line times** in the score follow the tracked beats; they assumed a steady tempo before.
- **File names.** "(Official Video)" and the like are dropped from the title and the output folder.
- **Evaluation.** `bass2sheet-eval` scores the tool on BabySlakh/Slakh2100 (exact notes and beats), MDB-bass-synth (exact f0 on real mixes) and RWC Popular (aligned MIDI on real recordings), with an error breakdown, a gate-vs-accuracy table and a baseline regression check.
- **Note starts** move to where the pluck begins to sound, at 1 ms resolution. Frame-level starts were 15–25 ms late.
- **Beats and downbeats** come from the Beat This! model. The built-in tracker chose the wrong pulse level on about half of the BabySlakh songs.
- **Repeated notes.** A held note is split where the string is plucked again. Driving eighth-note lines came out as single long notes on real recordings.
- **Separator choice.** When both separators were transcribed, the result with fewer failed gates is kept, then the one with more note starts confirmed by Basic Pitch. The report says which gates each attempt tripped.
- **Quality gates.** Thresholds are set from the evaluation runs, and five warnings at once count as a failure. The drum-bleed and synth-gap gates are gone: they fired on good results. A meter gate (downbeats that fit 4/4) is new; it and the eighth-note gate warn but never fail. Note coverage no longer penalizes a perfect transcription.
- **Memory.** CREPE's batch is bounded (it could need over 30 GB), and cached GPU memory is released between stages.
- **Inputs.** `--stems DIR` uses stems you already have. `--bass-stem`, `--beat-tracker`, `--crepe-model`, `--no-pdf`. The shortest accepted input is 10 s.
- **Score.** Lines start where the bass comes in.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for every stage and end-to-end tests on a synthesized song, with no recorded audio and no model download; an opt-in tier for real separation and the dataset regression.
