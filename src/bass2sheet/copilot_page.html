The mouse

Drag a notemoves it: up and down in pitch, left and right in time, in one go. It turns see-through while you hold it, with a line at its start and its end, so you can lay it on the picture.
Drag a note's endmoves where it starts or ends. The ends can be grabbed from a little outside the note too; the pointer turns into ↔.
Wheel, or pinchzooms in time around the pointer. A sideways swipe (or Shift with the wheel) moves through the song.
Drag the backgroundmoves the timeline, also up and down when the rows are taller than the window (or Alt with the wheel).
Drag in the rulermoves the playhead and lets you hear the spot under it as you go.
Clicka note selects it; the buttons under the timeline then act on it. Anywhere else puts the playhead there.
Shift-clickanother note selects all the notes in between.
Double-clickan empty spot adds a note there.
Rows − + Fitmakes the pitch rows shorter or taller, or fits them all in the window.
Pictureits colours, how strongly the faint tones show, and how solid the note boxes are. The page remembers them.
A letterover the timeline names the bars that sound alike. Click it and the other bars that sound like that one line up under the timeline, each with its own piece of the picture and its notes, orange where it is written unlike the bar you clicked. Hear each one as it is written and with the change, then change it there or leave it: nothing changes in a bar until you say so for that bar, and "Undo this one" takes it back.
After a fixthe bars that sound the same there and are written as this one was line up the same way.
An orange markunder a letter, or a dashed note, shows what a repeat has there: click to take it or leave it.
The song mapthe line over the timeline is the whole song: the patterns, the part you are looking at (the frame), the playhead, what you have worked through, and a dark line under every bar that sounds like the current one. Click or drag in it to go there.

The picture

The picture shows the steady tones of the isolated bass. A note is a stack of bars: the note itself at the bottom, its overtones above it, often stronger than the note. A bar that bends is a slide; the gap between two bars is where one note gives way to the next.
